## 2. What the measurements say about each row

- **Register machine (C1).** Not evaluated: the instruction-count comparison needed the user-space interpreters of sub-phase 6c, which was not run (`DEVIATIONS.md` D6.1 item 5).
- **In-place filtering and tap cost (C2, C3, C4).** The reject-curve and accept-curve verdicts are MIXED and INCONCLUSIVE and the flood shows a sender-generation bottleneck (`results/verdicts.md`); the structural point of the paper's row, that a non-matching packet need not be handed to a user-space reader, shows in the reject-curve table of `results/summary.md`: config B's capture process used 0.0% CPU and 1 context switch per second at 64 B while its filter dropped about 320,000 packets (the `filter drops (slot0)` column), and config A's capture process used 12.7 to 13.6% CPU and about 7,800 context switches per second at the same offered load (`capture-proc CPU %`, `proc cs/s`). That is a process-level observation at one offered load, not a per-byte copy measurement.
- **Buffer model.** Not measured separately. In the flood, config C (kernel filter, nobody reading) had 5,182,827 socket drops (mean of 4 runs) against 0.0% capture-process CPU (`results/summary.md`, Flood): the socket queue overflows when nothing reads it, as `docs/phase4_notes.md` (Counter semantics) found with the loader alone.
- **Safety (C5).** The verifier accepts a source-unsafe program it can prove safe (variant A) and rejects the two it cannot (`docs/phase5_analysis.md`).
